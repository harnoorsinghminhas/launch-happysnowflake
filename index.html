Welcome to Happy Snowflake. Here is a small thought for your day.

Every child is unique, like a snowflake. No two learn, play, or grow in quite the same way.

When it comes to superintelligence and artificial intelligence, you do not need to be an expert. You only need a few calm rules the whole family can follow.

Rule one. Be curious together. Ask your child to show you what they are using, and listen before you decide anything.

Rule two. A computer can be wrong, so we check.

Rule three. Some things we never share, like passwords, full names, or where we live.

And rule four. Devices rest at night, and so do we.

That is the start of a family rulebook. Small, kind, and easy to remember.

The seven rules

A short rulebook your family can actually keep

1We keep private things private.

Names, school, address, photos and feelings stay out of any AI program. Adults decide what is safe to share.

Ask: what is something about us that is nobody's business but ours?

2We check before we trust.

AI can sound sure and be wrong. For anything that matters, we check a second source together.

Ask: how could we check this together, with a book or a second source?

3We know what is real.

Pictures, voices and videos can be made by a computer. We pause and ask a grown-up when something feels off.

Ask: what clues tell us a picture or voice might be made by a computer?

4We ask a grown-up first.

New apps and tools get a quick look from an adult before anyone uses them.

Ask: which three things would you want me to look at first?

5We stay kind.

We speak to people and programs the way we want to be spoken to. Kind habits stay ours.

Ask: what is one kind thing we could say tomorrow?

6We still do our own thinking.

AI can help us learn, but we try first, then compare. Our own ideas come first.

Ask: what do you think first, before we look at any answer?

7We take screen-free time.

Time outdoors, with books, and with each other is part of growing up smart.

Ask: what would you like to do together with no screens this weekend?
